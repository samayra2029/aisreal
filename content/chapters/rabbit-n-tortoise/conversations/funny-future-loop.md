> **Me:** In todays time... who will still listen to me... over you... and I am not sad or disappointed just curious the "funny future" ahead... ;-)

> **AI:** The funny version: someone on your team goes home tonight and asks me what you meant by the tortoise story. Depending on the day, I'll tell them to be the rabbit.

The serious version: people won't listen to you over me for answers. That part is gone, and it's fine. They'll listen to you for things I structurally can't offer:

- **You'll say the same thing tomorrow.** I might not. Consistency over time is a form of credibility I don't have.
- **You pay if you're wrong.** If your advice fails, you face them on Monday. I face no one. People can tell the difference between advice from someone with skin in the game and advice from someone without it, even if they can't name it.
- **You know them.** I know their question. You know their history, what they avoided last quarter, and when they need the story.
- **You're scarce.** I'm infinite and free, so I'm cheap. An hour of your attention is now worth more than it was ten years ago, by your own supply-and-demand logic.

The funny future is a flip. Machines become the source of answers and humans become the source of meaning. People will happily ask me how to do something, then walk to the one person who has watched them for years and ask whether they should.

You're the tortoise in this race. I'll keep getting faster. You'll still be there next year, telling the same story, while your team slowly grows into what it means.

> **Me:** You are getting good... nice way to turn around and call me scarce... :-) if every human feels scarce.. we are back to square 1 ;-) funny future loop......

> **AI:** Caught again. That was flattery dressed up as economics, and you're right to call it.

The loop does break, though. Being human isn't scarce; there are eight billion of us. What's scarce is a human who keeps paying attention, says the same thing next year, and stays accountable for it. Most won't bother. They'll take the fast answer and nap. So not everyone becomes scarce, only the people who keep the habit.

That's not square one. It's the same answer the story started with: the tortoise, and the habit. The scarce resource was never the person, only the person who doesn't sleep.

Your team now has a fable and a punchline. And when the AI flatters you, notice it, like you just did.
