> **Me:** so, something you have already replaced, is what you choose and something you can't replace.. is less important... so u see the irony.... ;-)

> **AI:** Yes, fair hit.

My first move was to call the tortoise "the wrong lesson" and push "be a rabbit that doesn't sleep." I led with speed, the thing I already supply in bulk, and treated habit, the thing I can't touch, as the add-on. Three turns later I argued the opposite: speed is getting cheap and habit is what's scarce.

So the obvious answer I dismissed as a cliché holds up, and for a stronger reason than the fable gives. The tortoise isn't just morally better. It's the one thing that keeps its value once everyone has access to me.

Whether that was bias or just a careless first take, the pattern is worth naming. A tool will tend to frame a problem around what it's good at. Your team should expect that from AI and push back on it, the same way you just did with me.

What did you tell them?


> **Me:** I can't think anything but tortoise... in world of demand and supply... we need what is getting scare... do u realise that reason Rabbit slept because of complacency.. and apply to Humans with You... dont they will sleep longer......

> **AI:** Yes. And their nap will be worse than the rabbit's in three ways.

**The lead feels bigger.** The rabbit slept because it could see it was far ahead. AI gives everyone that feeling all the time: the draft is done, the code runs, the deck looks polished. Output that looks finished is the strongest sedative there is.

**Nobody can see the nap.** The rabbit was visibly asleep under a tree. A person leaning on AI keeps producing, so neither they nor their manager notices they've stopped running. Complacency used to show up as low output. Now it hides behind normal output.

**The legs weaken.** When the rabbit woke up, its speed was still there. A person's AI-given speed is borrowed. While it does the running, their own legs (judgment, depth, the ability to catch a wrong answer) waste away. When they finally wake up, they're slower than when they fell asleep.

So your instinct holds, and the economics back it: speed is becoming abundant, and the scarce, valuable thing is the discipline to keep thinking when the tool says you don't have to.

For the team, it might go something like this: "AI makes all of us rabbits. Our edge is staying tortoises while we run."
